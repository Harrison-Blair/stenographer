Dictation that stays on your machine.

Press a hotkey, speak, and your words land at your cursor. Local transcription runs on your own hardware, with no account. Your audio and transcripts never leave your machine.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Harrison-Blair/stenographer/main/scripts/quick-install.sh | bash -s -- --no-start

You need Linux on x86_64 or AArch64, a Wayland session, and a systemd user manager. The script downloads the latest release, verifies it against SHA256SUMS, and installs the user service for you. You do not need Python or build tools to install. The model build is a separate choice, and it needs your system python3-venv package.

How it works

  1. Your hotkeysRight Ctrl selects Agent; Right Alt selects General. Both can latch or be held, and Escape cancels without pasting.
  2. Your microphoneCaptured through PortAudio, with a quiet-audio gate so an accidental press does not paste noise into your work.
  3. Local transcriptionUnderdog Bark 3.0 Pro by Conway Research runs on your CPU through sherpa-onnx. You build the model once on your machine, and only when you ask for it.
  4. Local refinementOne local Ollama model serves two fixed profiles. Agent can rephrase, reorder, group ideas, and remove repetitions. Its prompt requires every distinct detail and authorization limit to survive. Agent trusts the model: it has no semantic or length-ratio checks, so names, numbers, negations, uncertainty, or authorization may change. It retains format, empty-output, truncation, and wrapper checks. General keeps its original cleanup, length, and numeral checks. Detected failures fall back to the formatted transcript.
  5. Your clipboardYour transcript is copied first. The paste fires only after the copy is confirmed and you have let go of the key. When Agent rewrites the text, the original is copied just before it, so your clipboard history keeps both.
  6. Your cursorA Shift+Insert chord through uinput types the text where you are working. About half a second later, your previous clipboard item is back.

What never leaves your machine

  • Your audio, your transcripts, your configuration, and your device and model names.
  • Logs carry timings and counts, never what you said.
  • The optional pill never sees your transcript or your raw audio.
  • Your dictation statistics are local and numeric, yours to export or delete.

The network exceptions

At most once every 24 hours, stenographer asks GitHub for the latest release tag. Metadata only, never a download, and silent if it fails. You get a desktop notification when a newer version exists. If you would rather it never asked, turn it off in your config:

[stenographer.feedback]
update_check = false

The cleanup pass is the other one, and it runs by default. Its default host is 127.0.0.1, so your transcript goes to a model on your own machine and no further. Point it somewhere else and your transcripts travel there — your config says so, and so does every daemon start. Turn it off in your config if you would rather skip it.

Install

  1. Check your essentials.

    You need a Wayland session, a systemd user manager, a PortAudio-backed microphone, read access to /dev/input/event* (usually by joining the input group), write access to /dev/uinput, and wl-copy from wl-clipboard. wl-paste, from the same package, lets it restore your previous clipboard item. Run stenographer doctor and it tells you what you are missing and how to fix it.

  2. Run the quick install above, or build it yourself using the build guide. 
    git clone https://github.com/Harrison-Blair/stenographer.git
cd stenographer
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -e ".[dev,build]"
scripts/install.sh --no-start
  3. Configure it and build the speech model when the wizard offers. 
    ~/.local/bin/stenographer setup --quick

    The build needs python3-venv once. Run sudo apt install python3-venv yourself, then build the model with stenographer model download --asr. It downloads about 1.6 GB, converts the model to int8 ONNX on your machine, and needs 10 GiB of free disk and about 10.2 GiB of RAM while it runs. On the test machine it took about 2 minutes. Speech-model settings from earlier releases stay in your config and are ignored.

    For clearer recognition, choose the intended input with stenographer devices, keep a close microphone about 10–20 cm away, and raise its level only until speech is clear without clipping. A close wired headset often beats a distant microphone.

  4. Start it and dictate. 
    systemctl --user start stenographer.service

    Use Right Ctrl for Agent or Right Alt for General. Hold either, speak, and release—or tap to latch and tap again when you are done.

Your configuration lives at ~/.config/stenographer/config.toml. When you save through the CLI, your comments and any settings it does not know about are kept. Restart the service to apply your changes.

CLI

stenographerOpen the three-screen terminal hub when stdin and stdout are terminals; otherwise show help. Press 1 for Record, 2 for Library, 3 for Settings, and ? for the keys of each screen. A sidebar shows navigation badges, an engine card with the model state and CPU and memory use, and a context card. Record has a REC panel, MIC and SYSTEM spectrum meters with a speech light, and mixed or split transcript views. Press space to start, enter to stop and save without leaving the hub, o to open the saved session in Library, m and s to toggle sources, d to choose the input device for this run, and v to switch views. Scroll with up/down, pageup/pagedown, home/end, or the mouse wheel; it follows new lines at the bottom. Library lists recordings, file sessions, and file jobs; press / to search transcripts, p to play, d or t to re-diarize or re-transcribe, x to export, and delete to delete or cancel a job. Press n to open Files, which opens on your saved recordings (or your home folder before the first recording), queues audio jobs, shows progress and previews, and exports transcripts. Settings has the General, Audio, Recognition, Live, and Checks tabs; ● marks an unsaved change, ctrl+s saves it with a backup, and the Vocabulary row edits the refine terms. Checks runs the system checks and setup tests. M4A and AAC input needs the system ffmpeg executable. See the hub guide.
stenographer runRun the daemon in your terminal, in the foreground.
stenographer liveOpen the standalone live app with its existing flags. Scroll the transcript with up/down, pageup/pagedown, home/end, or the mouse wheel. The view follows new lines at the bottom. After q, renamed labels stay on one voice and unnamed voices are numbered by first appearance; press q again to skip. When several people share your microphone, --diarize-mic (or live.diarize_mic = true) labels them Room 1, Room 2, and so on instead of You. See the live guide.
stenographer transcribe FILE [--raw] [--refine]Transcribe one of your audio files; add --raw to skip formatting, or --refine to clean it up.
stenographer model downloadDownload models explicitly. Use --asr, --refine, or --live to select them.
stenographer doctorCheck what your system needs and print the fixes.
stenographer devicesList your audio input devices.
stenographer setup [--quick | --default]Configure everything, only the common settings, or write the annotated defaults.
stenographer sounds [PACK]List, preview, or pick a whole sound pack.
stenographer statsSee your lifetime dictation totals; export them as JSON or CSV.
stenographer completion {bash,zsh,fish}Print a completion definition for your shell.

Add --help to any command for its full usage. See the user guide for configuration, sound packs, statistics, privacy, and troubleshooting.
