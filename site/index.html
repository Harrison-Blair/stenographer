Dictation that stays on your machine.

Press a hotkey, speak, and your words land at your cursor. Whisper runs on your own hardware, with no account and no network. Your audio and transcripts never leave your machine.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Harrison-Blair/stenographer/main/scripts/quick-install.sh | bash -s -- --no-start

You need Linux on x86_64 or AArch64, a Wayland session, and a systemd user manager. The script downloads the latest release, verifies it against SHA256SUMS, and installs the user service for you. You do not need Python or build tools.

How it works

  1. Your hotkeyRight Ctrl by default. Tap it to latch, or hold it, speak, and release. If you prefer plain hold or toggle, each is one setting away.
  2. Your microphoneCaptured through PortAudio, with a quiet-audio gate so an accidental press does not paste noise into your work.
  3. Local transcriptionfaster-whisper runs on your CPU or GPU. You download the model once, and only when you ask for it.
  4. Your clipboardYour transcript is copied first. The paste fires only after the copy is confirmed and you have let go of the key.
  5. Your cursorA Shift+Insert chord through uinput types the text where you are working. It stays on your clipboard too.

What never leaves your machine

  • Your audio, your transcripts, your configuration, and your device and model names.
  • Logs carry timings and counts, never what you said.
  • The optional pill never sees your transcript or your raw audio.
  • Your dictation statistics are local and numeric, yours to export or delete.

The one network exception

At most once every 24 hours, stenographer asks GitHub for the latest release tag. Metadata only, never a download, and silent if it fails. You get a desktop notification when a newer version exists. If you would rather it never asked, turn it off in your config:

[stenographer.feedback]
update_check = false

Install

  1. Check your essentials.

    You need a Wayland session, a systemd user manager, a PortAudio-backed microphone, read access to /dev/input/event* (usually by joining the input group), write access to /dev/uinput, and wl-copy from wl-clipboard. Run stenographer doctor and it tells you what you are missing and how to fix it.

  2. Run the quick install above, or build it yourself from source. 
    git clone https://github.com/Harrison-Blair/stenographer.git
cd stenographer
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -e ".[dev,build]"
scripts/install.sh --no-start
  3. Configure it, and download the model, about 1.5 GB, when the wizard offers. 
    ~/.local/bin/stenographer setup --quick
  4. Start it and dictate. 
    systemctl --user start stenographer.service

    Hold Right Ctrl, speak, and release. Or tap to latch, and tap again when you are done.

Your configuration lives at ~/.config/stenographer/config.toml. When you save through the CLI, your comments and any settings it does not know about are kept. Restart the service to apply your changes.

CLI

stenographer runRun the daemon in your terminal, in the foreground.
stenographer transcribe FILE [--raw]Transcribe one of your audio files; add --raw to skip formatting.
stenographer model downloadDownload the model you configured into your local cache.
stenographer doctorCheck what your system needs and print the fixes.
stenographer devicesList your audio input devices.
stenographer setup [--quick | --default]Configure everything, only the common settings, or write the annotated defaults.
stenographer sounds [PACK]List, preview, or pick a whole sound pack.
stenographer statsSee your lifetime dictation totals; export them as JSON or CSV.
stenographer completion {bash,zsh,fish}Print a completion definition for your shell.

Add --help to any command for its full usage. You get four sound packs: legacy, warm-desk, soft-electronic, and the default minimal-ui.
